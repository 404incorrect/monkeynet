🍌 Heist

One monkey, one neem tree, and a thirty-minute audit of the fiat system

Bulandshahr, Uttar Pradesh, India · May 31, 2026

Outside a courthouse, a monkey lifted a lawyer's bag holding roughly ₹2 lakh, carried it up a neem tree, and for about half an hour simply rained the currency notes back down while a crowd below scrambled to catch them. No demands, no manifesto. Just a small primate at the top of a tree, quietly demonstrating that a banknote is only worth something if everyone underneath agrees to keep chasing it. The video, predictably, went everywhere.

🐒 New Climbers

Seattle's canopy gets its first colobus in twenty-nine years, arriving all in white

Woodland Park Zoo, Seattle · born June 15, announced June 30, 2026

A black-and-white colobus monkey was born to parents Mokiki and Lewis, the first colobus the zoo has welcomed in twenty-nine years. Newborns arrive pure white and darken into their adult coloring over their first three months, so for a little while there's a small bright thing up in the branches that hasn't figured out yet how much it's going to change. First climb of many. Welcome, friend.

🌿 Retirement

A hundred and thirty retired research macaques are looking for a better branch to land on

United States · CDC · reopened June 20, reported July 1, 2026

After public pushback on a plan to send its retired rhesus and pigtail macaques to a single Texas sanctuary, the CDC reopened the process, posting a revised contract opportunity for facilities to bid on housing the 130 animals. It's paperwork, technically. But underneath the paperwork is a straightforward question about where a monkey who has already given a working life gets to spend the rest of it, and whether we can manage to get that part right. Worth watching where they end up.

✋ Hands Off

Someone pointed a laser at a baby monkey for the feed. The zoo is done asking nicely.

Ichikawa City Zoo, Japan · June 25, 2026

Punch, a baby monkey who'd gone viral, had a laser aimed into his enclosure by a visitor who then posted the clip online. Zoo officials warned it can cause real stress and even blindness, and said anyone caught doing it will be ejected on the spot. This is the extractive feed logic followed all the way to its meanest end: a living thing turned into raw material for a video. A monkey is not content. Punch is not content. Put the laser away.

Trafficking

A proboscis monkey turned up in Thailand, far from the only island that is home

Thailand · April 2026

An injured proboscis monkey, a species that lives nowhere on earth but Borneo, was found near a railway track in Thailand. It needed a finger and part of its tail amputated. There was no CITES paper trail explaining how it crossed a sea and a border, and experts say that absence points to one thing: illegal trade. A canopy is somewhere you climb because you want to. This is the opposite of that: a small life moved against its will, and hurt on the way. We're glad it was found. We wish it had never had to be.

The Canopy Itself

Four days of rain took roughly seven percent of the rarest great ape alive

Batang Toru, Indonesia · study published June 16, 2026

A study in Current Biology found that four days of extreme rain from Cyclone Senyar triggered landslides that killed an estimated 58 Tapanuli orangutans, a critically endangered great ape with fewer than 800 individuals left in the world. Not a monkey; a cousin, up in the same trees. There isn't a lighter way to hold a number like that, so we won't try. The canopy we like to talk about as a metaphor is, for them, an actual forest, and it is getting harder to live in. We're just here to make sure the number gets said out loud.
